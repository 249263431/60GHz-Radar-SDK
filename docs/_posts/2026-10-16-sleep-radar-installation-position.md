Sleep monitoring radar uses millimeter wave technology to detect breathing, heart rate, and body movement during sleep. Unlike wearable devices, it works without any physical contact. However, the accuracy of the data depends heavily on where the radar is installed. A wrong position can lead to weak signals, missed detections, or false alarms.

This article explains how to choose the best installation position for a sleep monitoring radar, based on the recommended setup in the product documentation.

## Why Installation Position Matters

Millimeter wave radar works by emitting a beam and analyzing the reflected signal. The beam has a limited coverage angle and a limited detection range. If the radar is too far from the person, or the beam does not cover the chest area, the breathing and heart rate signals will be too weak to detect.

Sleep monitoring radar needs to detect:

- Breathing rate (chest movement)
- Heart rate (chest vibration)
- Body movement (turning, getting in and out of bed)

All of these signals come from the chest area. The radar must be aimed at the chest to get reliable data.

## Recommended Installation Position

The recommended installation is **above the bed head, tilted downward at 30°–45°**.

This setup has several advantages:

- The radar covers the chest area directly
- The person does not need to wear anything
- The beam is not blocked by pillows or blankets
- The installation is hidden from view

### Installation Height

The radar should be installed **0.8–1 m above the bed surface**.

If the radar is too low, the beam may be blocked by the person's body. If it is too high, the signal becomes weak and the detection range shrinks.

### Tilt Angle

The tilt angle should be between **30° and 45°**.

- A smaller angle (closer to horizontal) may miss the chest area
- A larger angle (closer to vertical) may cause the beam to hit the bed frame or the wall

The goal is to make the main beam cover the chest area while avoiding obstacles.

## Maximum Detection Distance

The detection range depends on the type of signal:

| Signal Type | Maximum Distance |
|-------------|------------------|
| Breathing rate | ~1.5 m |
| Sleep detection | ~2.5 m |
| Human presence | ~2.5 m |

This means the radar must be installed within these distances from the person. If the bed is too large or the radar is installed too far away, the breathing signal may not be detected.

## What to Avoid

Several objects can interfere with the radar signal. Avoid installing the radar near:

- Air conditioners (internal motor causes false detection)
- Electric fans (moving blades cause false alarms)
- Curtains that move with wind
- Metal objects (reflect the signal)
- Water pipes or flowing water
- Pets that move around the bed

The radar is sensitive to any movement in the detection area. Even small movements, such as a fluttering curtain or a swinging fan, can cause false alarms.

## Installation Steps

1. Identify the main sleep area and the bed head position.
2. Mark a point 1 m above the bed head on the wall or ceiling.
3. Tilt the radar downward at 30°–45°.
4. Make sure the radar covers the chest area of the person lying on the bed.
5. Check for interference sources in the detection range and remove them.
6. Fix the radar in place and power it on.
7. Verify the sleep data in the app and adjust the angle if needed.

## Testing After Installation

After installation, test the radar with the following steps:

1. Lie on the bed in a normal sleeping position.
2. Stay still for at least 10 minutes.
3. Check if the radar reports breathing rate and heart rate.
4. Check if the sleep state changes from awake to light sleep.
5. If the data is unstable, adjust the tilt angle or installation height.

A simple test: cover the chest area with the radar beam and confirm the breathing waveform is visible in the app. If the waveform is flat or missing, the radar is not aimed correctly.

## Common Installation Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Solution |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Installed too far from the bed | Weak breathing signal | Move closer, within 1.5 m |
| Installed at a flat angle | Beam misses the chest | Tilt 30°–45° downward |
| Installed near an air conditioner | False alarms | Move away from air flow |
| Installed behind a curtain | Signal blocked | Remove obstruction |
| Installed too high | Reduced detection range | Lower to 0.8–1 m above bed |

## Summary

Sleep monitoring radar works best when installed above the bed head, tilted downward at 30°–45°, and positioned 0.8–1 m above the bed surface. Keep the radar within 1.5 m for breathing detection and 2.5 m for sleep detection. Avoid air conditioners, fans, moving curtains, and metal objects. After installation, test the radar for at least 10 minutes to confirm the breathing and heart rate signals are stable.

A correct installation is the foundation for accurate sleep data. If the data looks unstable, the first thing to check is the installation position and angle.
