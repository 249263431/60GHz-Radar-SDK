A recent conversation with a colleague — let's call him Lao Li — about the gap between Chinese and overseas sleep-product markets stuck with me. The gap is real, and it runs deeper than product features. At root it is about **consumption habits, willingness to pay, and market maturity**. The same sleep-monitoring technology meets two completely different market logics abroad and at home.

## Overseas: solution output plus secondary development, already at scale

In overseas markets — CES is a good window — many customers take our solution, whose core is analyzing **body-movement amplitude and sleep state**, and then build their own sleep-monitoring products on top of it through secondary development, selling to their local users.

The overseas market already has real scale. Customers place a high value on sleep data, are willing to invest in turning monitoring into a product, and treat sleep monitoring as part of **long-term health management** rather than a one-off purchase.

## China: operator projects dominate, consumer demand not yet scaled

Look back at the domestic sleep market and the picture is different. Aside from the occasional operator project, demand on the individual-user side is limited, and the market as a whole has not really taken off. In China, sleep monitoring still largely exists as **project- and institution-driven** demand, not yet a personal-scale consumer market.

## The root cause: different consumption habits

The decisive difference is habit:

- **Overseas users** are often willing to pay *monthly* for their health. They take ownership of their own wellbeing, want to understand their sleep and improve it, and treat health management as a continuous investment. Acceptance of subscription and ongoing-service models is high.
- **Chinese users** — especially some older adults — more often hold the attitude of "bear it for a while, only see a doctor when you can't bear it anymore." They will buy a health product or medical device *once*, but are reluctant to spend a few yuan a day on health, and find continuous payment hard to accept. Their tolerance for products and their payment expectations also differ markedly from overseas users.

| Dimension | Overseas | China |
|---|---|---|
| Go-to model | Solution + secondary development | Operator / institutional projects |
| Primary buyer | Product builders, local brands | Operators, institutions |
| Payment habit | Monthly subscription accepted | One-time hardware purchase |
| View of health | Proactive, continuous | Reactive ("treat when unbearable") |
| Market maturity | Already scaled | Not yet scaled |

## Conclusion: different habits and tolerance shape different demand

Because consumption habits, willingness to pay, and product tolerance differ, the **form of demand** differs fundamentally. Overseas markets lean toward "continuous monitoring plus subscription"; China currently leans toward "one-time hardware purchase."

So a single solution cannot win everywhere. Product form and business model must be designed **per market and per population**: emphasize continuous monitoring and the value of data services for overseas, and fit the one-time-hardware purchasing psychology for China.

*Honest caveat: this is drawn from conversations and field observation, not a formal market study. Both markets contain exceptions and are evolving; the "bear it first" pattern describes a tendency among some users, not a universal rule.*

## Takeaway

- The gap between Chinese and overseas sleep markets is about **habits and payment willingness**, not the technology.
- Overseas: customers build on our solution and accept subscription; the market is already scaled.
- China: demand is still operator- and institution-driven; consumer demand has not yet taken off.
- Overseas users pay continuously for health; many Chinese users prefer one-time hardware and resist daily fees.
- One solution does not fit both — design product and business model per market and per population.
